NFL Connector · Design direction

Moving the Chains

The game is well built and not yet fun. It has no way to lose, and its search box does the remembering for you. Fix those two things and the rest is polish — here is the polish, with the reasoning attached and the timings live so you can feel them.

Diagnosis

You cannot lose, so winning is not a relief

Wordle gives you six guesses. Connections gives you four mistakes. Both are generous enough that a competent player almost always wins — the limit is not really about difficulty, it is about making the win mean something. Right now NFL Connector lets you guess forever. Par measures efficiency, but efficiency is not tension: there is no moment where you feel the floor might drop.

The second problem is that the search dropdown lists each player's teams. Type four letters and the answer is on screen. That converts the game from recall (what do I know about the 2010 Steelers?) into recognition (which of these five rows looks right?). Recall is the fun. Recognition is the shortcut that eats it.

Principle

A daily puzzle needs a failure state it rarely reaches. The limit is not a difficulty knob — it is what converts a task into a game. Without it you have a form that validates input.

The fix already lives in the sport

You get four downs. Every wrong guess costs one. Convert a link and the chains move — downs reset to four. Burn all four on the same link and it is a turnover on downs. This is not a metaphor bolted on; it is the actual scoring structure of the sport the game is about, and every NFL fan already knows the rules. "Moving the chains" is literally what the player is doing.

Principle

When a mechanic can be borrowed from the subject itself, take it. A borrowed mechanic needs no tutorial, and it makes the game feel authored rather than templated.

Two easing choices carry the whole feel. Overshoot reads as mass. A plain ease-out says "the interface updated"; a curve that goes past its target and settles says "an object was thrown and landed". That is the difference between using an app and doing something. And fast attack, slow settle: the element should arrive almost instantly and then take its time coming to rest.

Wrong guess — 340ms, sharp, and then gone

Principle

Success animations may linger; failure animations must not. Make the player sit through their own mistake and they disengage. Sharp attack, sharp decay, out of the way — then let them move.

Sound: ship it muted, but ship it

Sound is the cheapest large gain in perceived polish and the fastest way to get a tab closed. Both are true, so: muted by default, one persistent toggle, three sounds only. Convert = a dry 60ms click. Miss = a soft low thud, never a buzzer — buzzers feel like punishment and punishment is not fun at 8am. Win = a three-note ascending stab.

Note the constraint your artifact runtime imposes: the CSP blocks external audio files, and base64 audio would bloat a page that already carries 1.2MB of roster data. Synthesise with WebAudio — zero payload, and it is about fifteen lines.

// convert: dry click. miss: low thud. win: ascending stab.
function blip(freq, ms, type, gain) {
  const ac = window.__ac || (window.__ac = new AudioContext());
  const o = ac.createOscillator(), g = ac.createGain();
  o.type = type; o.frequency.value = freq;
  g.gain.setValueAtTime(gain, ac.currentTime);
  g.gain.exponentialRampToValueAtTime(0.0001, ac.currentTime + ms / 1000);
  o.connect(g).connect(ac.destination);
  o.start(); o.stop(ac.currentTime + ms / 1000);
}
convert = () => blip(880, 60, 'square', .05);
miss    = () => blip(150, 140, 'sine',  .08);
win     = () => [523, 659, 784].forEach((f, i) =>
            setTimeout(() => blip(f, 180, 'triangle', .06), i * 110));

2 · Visual identity

Sunday night broadcast, not sports database

The current palette — newsprint neutrals, condensed type — is tasteful and reads as a reference site. It will not stand out as a screenshot in a group chat, which is where this game lives or dies.

The proposal is the broadcast graphics language: the scorebug, the chain gang, and above all the yellow first-down line — the most recognisable graphic in American sport, owned by no team, and unmistakably NFL without touching a single piece of league IP. Dark ground, because broadcast graphics live on dark and because a dark card is conspicuous in a feed of light ones.

One saturated colour, spent in one place. Everything except the marker is desaturated. Wordle owns green, Connections owns purple; this game should own broadcast yellow, and it only earns that if yellow never appears on a button, a border, or a heading that is not about progress.

Team colours are the exception, and they are the good kind: they are data. The page's colour comes from the teams your chain routed through, so no two finished puzzles look alike.

No icon library. Every mark should be a football object drawn in CSS or SVG: four squares for the down box, a 3px yellow rule for the first-down line, interlocking rounded rects for a chain link. Borrowed iconography is where sports apps start looking like every other app.

3 · The chain as a board

A drive chart, not a list

The current chain is a stack of cards. It reads as a form — fill this, then fill that. The demo above is the redesign, and four things change:

  • The endpoints are zones, not cards. Start and target get full-bleed tinted bands with their own tags; your links get plain plates. One glance tells you what is fixed and what is yours.
  • One continuous rail runs the whole drive, tinted per segment by the team that made the connection. A finished puzzle becomes a stripe of team colours — the thing worth screenshotting.
  • Par is shown as empty sockets. Wordle shows five blank tiles before you type; you know the shape of the answer. Two dashed sockets do the same job here, and the next one pulses. Filling a socket is more satisfying than appending to a list because a socket is a question already asked.
  • The first-down marker advances down the board on every conversion. It is the one element whose only job is to say you moved.
Principle

Give progress a physical object that moves. A counter incrementing is information; a marker travelling down a field is an event. Players remember events.

Growing the board without measuring heights in JS — the grid-template-rows trick animates to intrinsic height, which max-height hacks never do cleanly:

.row       { display: grid; grid-template-rows: 0fr;
             transition: grid-template-rows 320ms cubic-bezier(.16,.9,.3,1); }
.row.is-in { grid-template-rows: 1fr; }
.row > div { overflow: hidden; min-height: 0; }   /* both required */

Component structure to hand over:

<Board>
  <Zone kind="start"  player />        // blue band, START tag
  <Row>                                // repeats, animates open
    <Segment team season />            // tinted rail + chip
    <Plate player />
  </Row>
  <Socket state="next|pending" />      // one per remaining par link
  <MarkerLine />                       // advances on convert
  <Zone kind="end" player />           // yellow band, TARGET tag
</Board>

4 · The share

Your current share spoils the puzzle

It prints both players' names. Anyone who reads it in a group chat before playing has had the puzzle handed to them, so the people most likely to see your result are the ones it ruins. Wordle's grid is the canonical solution: it conveys the entire experience and leaks nothing.

Generated from the demo above — play it and watch this change

Reading left to right it is a drive: 🔵 start, one square per link, 🟡 target. Green squares are links converted on the first try, yellow ones took a miss. Length shows efficiency, colour shows cleanliness, and no name or team ever appears.

  • Number the puzzles, do not date them. "#142" implies a series with history and is easier to compare than a date. Wordle did this deliberately.
  • Make perfection visibly rare. Par, no misses, no hints swaps every square for 🏈. A marker that only shows up occasionally is something to chase — scarcity in the share is the aspiration.
  • Use navigator.share() on mobile with clipboard as fallback. The native sheet roughly doubles completed shares versus copy-then-paste.
Principle

A share has three jobs: spoil nothing, be recognisable at a glance while scrolling, and invite comparison. Plain numbers fail the second — text does not catch the eye in a feed. That is why the grid, not the score, is what made Wordle spread.

5 · Ritual & retention

Five things, and nothing else

MechanicMinimum versionWhy it works
Countdown"Next drive in 07:42:19", ticking, on the win card.Turns "come back tomorrow" into an appointment with a time on it. Highest return for the least code, and it is information rather than a nag.
Streak + bye weekCurrent and best streak. One miss per week does not break it.A broken streak is the top cause of permanent churn — the sunk value vanishes and so does the player. Forgiveness keeps them without manipulating them, and "bye week" is the sport's own word for it.
Four statsPlayed, win %, streak, par rate. One small bar chart of links over par.Stats pages are where daily games bloat. Wordle has four numbers and one histogram. More slows the post-win moment, which is the moment you want to end on a high.
One puzzle a dayNo playing ahead. Free play is framed as practice and excluded from stats.Scarcity is the entire engine of the genre. If unlimited play counts for anything, the daily stops being an occasion and the ritual collapses.
No archive yetShip without it.An archive converts a 30-day retention curve into one long session followed by churn. Add it only once daily retention is proven.
Principle

For a daily game the product is the ritual, not the session. Optimise for "came back tomorrow" and never for "stayed longer today" — which inverts most engagement design, and is exactly why the genre feels good to play.

The line you asked about — where retention becomes manipulation — is worth naming: no push nags, no "your streak dies in 2 hours", no coins or XP, no leaderboard by default. The genre's whole appeal is that it respects your time and lets you go. Anything designed to extend a session works against the brand.

Sequence

If you only do three things

Four downs makes it a game. Hiding teams in the dropdown makes it a memory game. The share grid without names makes it spread. Everything above this line is worth doing; those three are why it would be worth someone's Tuesday morning.
